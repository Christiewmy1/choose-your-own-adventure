# HuskyAdvisor Major Comparison Guide

## Purpose

This document supports one of HuskyAdvisor's core project goals: helping UWB students think through the difference between the UWB pathways HuskyAdvisor currently supports in a more structured way.

## Why This Matters

A lot of students do not need a perfect major prediction. They need a simpler answer to questions like:

- which path is more software-heavy?
- which one fits embedded systems best?
- which one is better if I want a business/technology blend?
- which one prepares me for companies like Boeing, Microsoft, or T-Mobile?

This guide exists to make that comparison more concrete.

## Current Supported Pathways

HuskyAdvisor now supports these UWB majors at different levels of depth:

- `CSSE`
- `Applied Computing`
- `Electrical Engineering`
- `Computer Engineering`
- `Data Visualization`
- `Business Administration` for technology-facing MIS / analytics / innovation-style planning

The first three remain the deepest pathways. The others are supported through organized shared-course mappings and honest scope notes.

## High-Level Comparison

### CSSE

Best for students who want:
- software engineering depth
- systems and backend work
- project-heavy coding experience

Strongest signals:
- software engineering
- operating systems
- networking/distributed systems
- testing and reliability

### Applied Computing

Best for students who want:
- flexible applied tech skills
- web, data, or business-technology roles
- a broader application focus rather than only low-level systems

Strongest signals:
- applied software
- information systems
- business/data alignment
- cross-functional technology work

### EE

Best for students who want:
- hardware
- embedded systems
- robotics or device-level engineering
- signals, control, and physical-system thinking

Strongest signals:
- circuits and systems
- embedded integration
- hardware/software interaction

### Newer Adjacent Pathways

#### Computer Engineering

Best for students who want:
- hardware-software bridge work
- firmware or architecture-aware engineering
- strong overlap between EE and CSS pathways

#### Data Visualization

Best for students who want:
- analytics
- dashboards and reporting
- data communication plus computing support

#### Business Administration

Best for students who want:
- MIS and business systems work
- analytics or digital transformation roles
- a business/technology bridge rather than a pure engineering path

## Honest Limitation

HuskyAdvisor should not claim that one major is universally “better.” The right use is:

- compare the strengths of each path
- connect them to student goals
- show which courses and company pathways line up best
